## In this episode

SAM: Welcome to Pass the Architect, Foundations retake. This is episode thirteen, exam day.

ALEX: The morning of. No new material?

SAM: None. By the end you'll know what to check before you leave, what to write on the noteboard in the first few minutes, how to read the countdown clock, what each item format asks of you, and what to do when an item rattles you.

ALEX: And you passed the Professional exam on the same method, so this is a routine, not a gamble.

SAM: Exactly. Let's walk it in order. [sting]

## Before you leave

SAM: Photo I D, government-issued, unexpired, with the name exactly as it is on your registration. Put it by the door now.

ALEX: Breakfast?

SAM: A proper one, and water. Concentration drops in the last half hour, and the last twenty items count the same as the first twenty.

ALEX: Anything to revise on the way?

SAM: Nothing new. If you want something in your ears, replay the anti-patterns from the final drill. Arrive early. A late arrival past the window forfeits the fee.

ALEX: And at the centre?

SAM: Phone, watch and headphones go in the locker. You accept the confidentiality agreement on screen before the first item. After the exam, the questions, options and scenarios stay with you. [sting]

## The noteboard

SAM: You get a noteboard and a marker. First, ask the administrator whether you may write before the clock starts. If not, write it in the first four minutes of the exam. The flag reserve covers that.

ALEX: And what goes on it?

SAM: The sheet you practised. I'll read it a line at a time. Write along, or say it back.

SAM: Line one. Root, smallest, guarantees. An order or a rule that must hold lives in code, not in the prompt.

ALEX: Root, smallest, guarantees.

SAM: Line two, the pace. One minute fifty an item. The clock counts down in hours and minutes, so the checkpoints are time left. Item twenty at one hour twenty-three. Item forty at forty-seven minutes. Item sixty at ten minutes.

ALEX: One twenty-three, forty-seven, ten.

SAM: Then each item, four steps. Find the constraint words: NOT, first, most, every, money. Find the component the trigger touched. Remove the root cause; do not only watch it. And ask whether the guide would pick it, or your last team.

ALEX: Constraint, component, root, guide.

SAM: Then strike these, use these. Stop on text or an iteration cap: use the stop reason. A prompt for a rule or an order: use a hook or a prerequisite gate. "Operation failed", or empty marked as success: use an error category and a retryable flag. Escalate on sentiment or confidence: escalate on request, on a policy gap, on no progress.

ALEX: Self-review, or a bigger model for a big diff?

SAM: A fresh instance, and one pass per file. Batch for a blocking check, or a retry for data the document lacks: synchronous, and a null. Eighteen tools on one agent, team config in a home directory, or a secret in the shared file: four or five tools per role, project scope, and an environment variable.

ALEX: And the box at the bottom.

SAM: Left panel changes, read it again. A flag you have never heard of, strike it. [sting]

## Item formats

SAM: Three shapes to expect. Multiple choice, one answer. Multiple response, where the item says how many to pick. Read that number first.

ALEX: And the third?

SAM: At the Professional exam you met items with four or five statements, each with its own dropdown of two or three choices. They may not appear at Foundations, but if they do, every statement is its own small item. There is no throwaway option, so judge each one alone.

ALEX: And no domain label on the items, either.

SAM: Right. Nothing tells you which domain you are in. The scenario frame in the left column is the context you get, so when it changes, read it in full before the next item. [sting]

## When an item rattles you

ALEX: What if I hit one I can't crack?

SAM: Pick your best answer, flag it, and move on. A blank never scores, and a flagged answer can still be right.

ALEX: And if I hit three in a row?

SAM: Pens down for one breath. Then read the next stem's last line first. Most hard items get easier once you know exactly what is being asked: first step, most effective, or NOT.

ALEX: And if I'm behind at a checkpoint?

SAM: Take the best answer on anything past two minutes, and flag it. Do not grind. The reserve is there for exactly that. [sting]

## The last ten minutes

SAM: Open Review. Go to flagged items first, then unanswered. Fill every blank.

ALEX: Should I change answers?

SAM: Only for a reason you can point to in the stem: a constraint word you missed, a number you misread. A new feeling is not a reason.

ALEX: And when it ends?

SAM: Submit, and go and eat something good. That is the routine: I D, breakfast, noteboard, countdown, constraint words, flag and move on, Review at the end.

ALEX: Root, smallest, guarantees.

SAM: Good luck.
