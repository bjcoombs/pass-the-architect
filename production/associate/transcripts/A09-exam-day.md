## In this episode

SAM: Welcome to Pass the Architect, Associate Foundations. This is episode nine, exam day.

ALEX: The morning of. Nothing new?

SAM: Nothing new. By the end you'll know what to check before you start, what to write on the noteboard, how to read the countdown clock, what each item format asks of you, and how to use the last ten minutes.

ALEX: A routine, then.

SAM: A routine. Let's walk it in order. [sting]

## Before you start

SAM: Photo I D: government-issued, unexpired, with the name exactly as it appears on your registration. Put it by the door now.

ALEX: And if I'm testing at home?

SAM: You stay in view of the webcam for the whole session. The desk is clear: no notes, no books, no phone, no second monitor. Nobody else talks to you. Phones, smart watches, headphones and anything that records are prohibited wherever you sit.

ALEX: And at a test centre?

SAM: The same rules, and your things go in a locker. Either way, be ready early. Arriving after the late window forfeits the fee.

SAM: Before the first item you accept a confidentiality agreement. Decline it and the session ends, with no refund. Afterwards, the questions, options and scenarios stay with you.

ALEX: Breakfast?

SAM: A proper one, and water. The last twenty items count the same as the first twenty. [sting]

## The noteboard

SAM: If Pearson VUE gives you a noteboard or scratch paper, ask whether you may write before the clock starts. If not, write it in the first few minutes. The ten-minute reserve covers that.

SAM: Here is the sheet. I'll read it a line at a time. Write along, or say it back.

SAM: Line one, the method. Ask, stakes, step, strike.

ALEX: Ask, stakes, step, strike.

SAM: Line two, the pace. The clock shows time left. Item twenty at one hour twenty-three. Item forty at forty-seven minutes. Item sixty at ten minutes.

ALEX: One twenty-three, forty-seven, ten.

SAM: Read them as a gap. Finish item twenty with one hour thirty showing and you are seven minutes ahead: keep them for the flags at the end. Finish it with one hour fifteen and you are eight behind: from then on, anything slow gets your best answer and a flag.

ALEX: Ahead, bank it. Behind, flag faster.

SAM: Line three, strike these, use these. A confident citation, figure or name: check it at the source. The top model for high-volume simple work: the faster, lower-cost one. Telling Claude not to keep something: fix the data before it goes in, as the policy says.

ALEX: And skipping the task?

SAM: The proportionate step that lets it go ahead. Rewording to sound better: fix whether it's right, not how it sounds. Pasting everything in: only what the task needs.

ALEX: And a long chat that has lost the thread?

SAM: Summarise it and start fresh, or keep what must persist in a Project. And a technical build that is beyond your role: escalate it to an Architect or a Developer. That is not giving up.

ALEX: And the box at the bottom.

SAM: Fluent is not correct. Sized to the stakes, not the maximum. [sting]

## Item formats

SAM: Three shapes. Multiple choice, one answer. Multiple response, where the item says how many to pick. Read that number first, then judge each option alone.

ALEX: And the dropdowns.

SAM: Four or five statements, each with two or three choices. Every statement is its own small item, and none is a throwaway. Call each one on its own merits.

ALEX: And no domain labels.

SAM: None. A governance item looks like any other workplace story, so read the stakes in every stem: who receives it, and what rule applies. [sting]

## When an item rattles you

ALEX: What if I hit one I can't crack?

SAM: Best answer, flag it, move on. A blank never scores.

ALEX: And when should I slow down?

SAM: When a stem names a policy, personal data, or someone who will rely on the output: a compliance team, a client, a customer. Those words carry the item, and they are the ones a tired eye skips. Spend your banked time there.

ALEX: Three hard ones in a row?

SAM: Pen down for one breath. Then read the next stem's last line first. Most items get easier once you know what is being asked: most appropriate, best fits, first, or NOT.

ALEX: And if I'm behind at a checkpoint?

SAM: Anything past two minutes gets your best answer and a flag. Do not grind. The reserve is there for exactly that. [sting]

## The last ten minutes

SAM: Open the review screen. Flagged items first, then anything unanswered. Fill every blank.

ALEX: Should I change answers?

SAM: Only for a reason you can point to in the stem: a number to pick that you misread, a policy you skipped past, a word like "first". A new feeling is not a reason.

ALEX: And when it ends?

SAM: Submit, and go and eat something good. The result is pass or fail with a scaled score; the domain percentages are for your information. That's the routine: I D, breakfast, noteboard, countdown, the four steps, flag and move on, review at the end.

ALEX: Ask, stakes, step, strike.

SAM: Good luck.
