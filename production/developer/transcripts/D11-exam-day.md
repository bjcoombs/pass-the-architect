## In this episode

SAM: Welcome to Pass the Architect, Developer Foundations. This is episode eleven, exam day.

ALEX: No new material?

SAM: None. By the end you'll know what to check before you leave, what to write on the noteboard, where the countdown checkpoints fall, what each item format asks of you, and how to spend the last ten minutes.

ALEX: A routine, then.

SAM: A routine. In order. [sting]

## Before you start

SAM: Photo I D: government-issued, unexpired, and the name exactly as it is on your registration. Put it by the door now.

ALEX: And if I'm testing online?

SAM: You stay in view of the webcam for the whole session, and the desk is clear: no notes, no books, no phone, no second monitor. Phones, smart watches and headphones are prohibited either way. At a test centre they go in the locker.

ALEX: Timing?

SAM: Arrive early, or log in early. Missing the late-arrival window forfeits the fee. Eat a proper breakfast and drink water. The last ten items count the same as the first ten.

ALEX: Anything to revise on the way?

SAM: Nothing new. If you want something in your ears, replay the recap from episode ten. Tells, not facts: the word in the stem and where it sends you.

ALEX: Anything before the first item?

SAM: You accept a confidentiality agreement on screen. Afterwards, the questions, options and scenarios stay with you. [sting]

## The noteboard

SAM: Pearson VUE sets what you may write on. At a centre that's usually a noteboard or scratch paper. Ask whether you may write before the clock starts. If not, write it in the first three minutes. The reserve covers that.

ALEX: And what goes on it?

SAM: Four lines and a box. I'll read them. Write along, or say them back.

SAM: Line one, the method. Count, constraint, mechanism, strike.

ALEX: Count, constraint, mechanism, strike.

SAM: Line two, the pace. Fifty-three items, about two minutes each, ten minutes in reserve. The clock counts down, so write time left. Item eighteen at one hour twenty-three. Item thirty-five at forty-seven. Item fifty-three at ten.

ALEX: One twenty-three, forty-seven, ten.

ALEX: Why write the pace down? I can do the sums in my head.

SAM: Not at item thirty-four with a dropdown open and the clock in hours and minutes. Written down, a checkpoint is a glance. Worked out, it's another item.

SAM: Line three, strike these. Faster or bigger. A polite instruction. Pasted data.

ALEX: Faster or bigger, polite instruction, pasted data.

SAM: Line four, constraint to mechanism. Cost first, no rush: batch. Untrusted content: keep it apart, and enforce with a guardrail or hook. Several applications: one M C P server. Must never happen: a control in code, not a line in the prompt. Output another system parses: structured output, then validate it, and never trust it because it sounds confident.

ALEX: Batch, separate and enforce, M C P server, control, validate.

SAM: And the box at the bottom. Dropdowns: each statement alone. A flag or feature you have never heard of: strike it. [sting]

## Item formats

SAM: Three shapes. Multiple choice, one answer. Multiple response, where the item says how many to pick. Read that number before the stem.

ALEX: Then judge each option on its own.

SAM: True for this stem, or not. Pick exactly the number asked, and treat one wrong pick as costing the whole item.

ALEX: And the dropdowns.

SAM: Four or five statements, each with two or three choices. No choice is a throwaway, so give each statement its own small read of the stem. They take longer, and the two-minute pace absorbs that if you don't grind. If one statement stumps you, settle the others first; they often show you what the item is testing.

ALEX: And no domain on the screen.

SAM: Right. Nothing tells you which domain you're in. A Claude Code item may be testing configuration, or hooks. Read the stem, not a heading. [sting]

## When an item stalls you

ALEX: One I can't crack?

SAM: Best answer, flag it, move on. A blank never scores, and a flagged answer can still be right.

ALEX: And three hard ones in a row?

SAM: Pens down for one breath. Then read the next stem's last line first. Most hard items ease once you know whether it asks for best fit, first step, or NOT.

ALEX: Behind at a checkpoint?

SAM: Answer and flag anything past two minutes. The reserve is there for exactly that.

ALEX: And if I'm well ahead?

SAM: Don't spend it on second guesses mid-paper. Keep the pace and bank the time for Review. [sting]

## The last ten minutes

SAM: Open Review. Flagged items first, then any unanswered. Fill every blank.

ALEX: Should I change answers?

SAM: Only for a reason you can point to in the stem: a constraint word you missed, a number you misread. A new feeling is not a reason.

ALEX: And when it ends?

SAM: Submit. The result is pass or fail with a scaled score, and seven hundred and twenty passes. Then go and eat something good.

SAM: That's the routine. I D, breakfast, noteboard, checkpoints, constraint words, flag and move on, Review at the end.

ALEX: Count, constraint, mechanism, strike.

SAM: Good luck.
